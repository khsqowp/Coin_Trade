# Preregistration: per-position stop-loss study

Timestamp (UTC): 2026-11-13T04:20:34Z. This document is frozen before any SL backtest cell is run. Subsequent clarifications are dated addenda; results will not change these definitions.

## Question and scope

Test whether per-position stop-loss overlays improve the unchanged ENV-A momentum rotation strategy. Entry ranking, signal lookback, regular rebalance cadence, K, dollar-neutral target sizing, funding, fees, kill-switch, and BTC crash signal are inherited from `rs_final_engine` / `rs_tp_engine`. Research is offline and reads only `.rs_cache` data. No live code or runtime is in scope.

## Baseline, data, and timing

- ENV-A: lookback 14 days; rebalance every 3 days; K=8 longs and 8 shorts; leverage 4; deploy 0.95 (target gross 3.8x); kill at 35% drawdown from HWM; 14-day cooldown; 0.1% per-side cost; real funding; minimum notional 0; initial equity 1; BTC 14-day return below -10% crash flat. Hourly panel has 45 current-listing symbols, 2022-01-01 through 2026-10-09, with H/L/C and no open.
- Evaluate rebalance phases 0, 1, and 2 and report the equal-weight phase mean and worst phase. Windows: IS 2022-01-01..2024-06-30; OOS 2024-07-01..panel end. Also report calendar years 2022–2026.
- Orders/rebalances are at daily close (hour 23). A position opened on close of day d is first stop-eligible from d+1 hour 0. Stops scan every hourly bar on every held day, including between rebalances. Crash flag for day i is evaluated at day i close using the base engine's flag[i]; a transition forces a rebalance/flatten at that close.
- Same-side kept positions retain quantity and the original average entry price, consistent with live `apply_targets`; the stop reference does not reset at rebalance. The `resize_all`/constant-weight path is not used.

## Stop definitions, execution, and accounting

Underlying adverse threshold s is measured from the original entry price. Long stop: hourly low <= entry*(1-s). Short stop: hourly high >= entry*(1+s). At 4x, the unlevered price move maps to approximate isolated margin PnL of -4s: 3%, 5%, 7.5%, 10%, 12.5%, 15%, 20%, 25%, 30% adverse corresponds to -12%, -20%, -30%, -40%, -50%, -60%, -80%, -100%, -120% of initial position margin. At s >=25%, label the stop liquidation-level or beyond; cross-margin liquidation can occur before a modeled stop.

Fill mode 0 fills at the stop threshold. Mode 1 (PRIMARY) fills 0.30% further adversely than threshold. Mode 2 fills at the trigger bar close. Because the panel has no open, gap-through is defined without inventing one: for a long, if the whole bar is below the stop (bar high < stop), base fill is bar high; otherwise stop. For a short, if the whole bar is above the stop (bar low > stop), base fill is bar low; otherwise stop. Apply adverse mode-1 slippage after this base choice, then clamp the fill to [low, high]. Mode 2 uses bar close, which lies within the panel bar range. This is an explicit range-clamped approximation, not an exchange queue model.

For each held position, the first trigger hour among 24 bars wins. When both SL and optional TP trigger in the same bar, SL wins; when triggers occur in different hours, the earlier hour wins. The trigger bar's adverse extreme still contributes to intraday cross-margin worst equity. After its trigger hour, the closed position contributes the realized fill PnL and no later funding. Apply the ordinary 0.1% exit cost to exit notional at fill. Freed notional remains cash until the next regular rebalance. `reentry` allows normal ranking then; `lockout` excludes a stopped symbol at the next rebalance only, clearing the ban after that rebalance and on kill.

Stop trade records include symbol, side, entry day, exit day/hour, exit type (rebalance/SL/TP/kill), net position PnL, entry and exit price, entry notional, equity at entry, and adverse underlying excursion. Whipsaw is the share of stopped positions for which close price recovers past entry within 3 or 7 calendar days after exit (long close >= entry; short close <= entry). Hit rate is the complement of 7-day whipsaw: stopped positions that do not recover within 7 days. Report average realized loss per stop in underlying return and as a fraction of equity-at-entry. Stops/year are separately counted long and short.

The base engine's liquidation check is intraday cross-margin: compare worst hourly equity (including funding) with maintenance margin based on hourly highs. It runs before stop realization in the trigger day. The kill switch uses daily close equity and HWM drawdown and is processed after stops/close valuation in the TP engine; report liquidation events and kill events by cell and baseline, and state which mechanism acted first whenever an SL-threshold cell has a liquidation. A stop cannot be described as preventing liquidation if the base engine's intraday liquidation test has already fired.

## Crash-filter variants and flat-window counterfactual

Crash modes: 0 disables flatting; 1 uses the live full-book flatten; 2 empties longs but continues the normally ranked short book with usual per-position target notional. On flag transitions, mode 2 also forces rebalance. Mode 1 is default for the primary grid. Reference rows compare mode 0 and mode 2 each with no stop and the IS-selected best both-side reentry stop; these are explanatory only and will not be used to recommend changing the live filter.

For each maximal run of flagged days, report start/end flag days and BTC 14-day move. For each side, counterfactual positions are selected by normal ranking at the close before the first flat decision (or at the forced flatten rebalance if that is the engine transition), sized at ENV-A per-leg notional, then held until the close on the final flag day or the next regular rebalance, whichever comes first. Report notional-weighted underlying PnL including funding as % of starting equity; before and after 0.2% round-trip cost. Compare with crash-off backtest equity change over the same dates. These are fixed-book counterfactuals, not a second strategy simulation.

5. Crash reference rows: crash mode 0 and 2, each with no SL and the IS-selected best both-side reentry SL. Mode 1 primary rows are shared when identical.

The report will give this exact deduplicated count and the raw planned family counts. Three phase executions per cell are not three unique trials. ## Metrics and selection

For each cell and phase, calculate CAGR (365-day annualization), close MDD, intraday MDD under the existing `window_metrics` convention, Calmar_i = CAGR / intraday MDD, daily Sharpe × sqrt(365), kill events/year, liquidations, SL events/year long and short, average stop loss, 3/7-day whipsaw and hit rate, average holding days, gross exposure and flat share, turnover, and total cost / average equity / year. Report phase mean and worst phase. Report IS, OOS, full period, each calendar year, and side/symbol trade contributions. For selected candidates, list the five largest contributing stop-related events and compare with baseline over the same calendar window; quantify top-five-day and top-five-event share of measured improvement.

Select within each stop family using IS phase-mean Calmar_i, subject to IS asset-loss <=15%. Asset-loss means candidate IS ending-wealth multiple is at least 85% of baseline IS ending-wealth multiple (phase mean). Top candidates are selected solely by this criterion. OOS and annual results are held out for diagnosis.

For each grid family, label helps/hurts/noise. Neighboring levels are the tested stop level plus up to two adjacent grid positions on each side, clipped to the family. Helps requires at least 80% of neighbors to improve Calmar_i versus the matching baseline in both IS and OOS; hurts requires at least 80% to worsen Calmar_i in both windows; otherwise noise. The previous TP-study CAGR tolerance is 0.9x baseline: any PASS-style claim also requires CAGR >=0.9× baseline in both windows. State raw trial count and a Bonferroni-style caution (nominal 0.05/count) for selection; a DSR-style haircut may be added but will not replace transparent trial accounting.

## STX-type event analysis and limitations

An STX-type event is a baseline holding spell whose adverse hourly underlying excursion from entry reaches at least 20% before the position exits at rebalance, crash, kill, or panel end. Count distinct `(symbol, side, entry day)` across phases and report phase-specific counts as a sensitivity. For each stop level, report whether and when it would stop, realized underlying loss, its 3/7-day whipsaw status, and the baseline spell's unstopped outcome at its actual next exit.

The sample is one historical path and selection spans many correlated trials. TP research found that a handful of flash-crash days materially influenced outcomes; quantify concentration directly. The 45 symbols are today's listings, so delisted and bottom-ranked assets are absent; this survivorship omission understates short squeeze/delist risk and can distort long/short rankings. Stop fills are range-clamped approximations; liquidation and stop ordering follow the engine's intraday cross-margin check. These limits constrain any live inference.
