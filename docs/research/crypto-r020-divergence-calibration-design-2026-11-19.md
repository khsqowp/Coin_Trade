# R020 exact-family synthetic calibration — design review draft

**Status: archived design review; superseded as the active research sequence by the micro-first Phase 0–8 program and the 2026-11-19 next-stage screen. No R020 simulation or market-label access occurred.** This document does not authorize opening divergence outcomes and does not replace the frozen divergence rules or success gates. The review fixed the claim-null definition, WFA selection contract, paired/full-rule error relationship, and data denominators. The proposal below supplies reproducible arbitrary synthetic constants, but it does not establish registered mixed-null truth or make the required full campaign computationally feasible. Do not resume the interrupted macro-filter-first WFA under this R020 identity.

## Why R020 is required

The existing R019 synthetic family has 24 claims / 48 contrasts generated as 12 streams × 2 horizons. The divergence study has 20 claims / 40 contrasts generated as 2 oscillators × 5 timeframes × 2 horizons. The difference is structural, not merely array size: the claims share underlying minute-price paths, same-anchor events, and overlapping horizon windows. The old R019 calibration therefore cannot be transferred or resized.

## Frozen target family

| Dimension | Values | Count |
|---|---|---:|
| Oscillator | RSI(14); MACD histogram (12,26,9) | 2 |
| Native timeframe | 1m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 60m | 5 |
| Horizon | 10 and 20 native bars, separate claims | 2 |
| Baselines per claim | Same-anchor momentum; SHA-256 fair direction | 2 |
| Family | oscillator × timeframe × horizon | 20 claims / 40 paired contrasts |

The two horizons of a stream must reuse the same issued anchor and the same simulated future path. They must not be sampled as independent Bernoulli outcomes. Claim-specific stop/target geometries can differ, so `target_first_10 => target_first_20` must not be assumed unless the simulated barriers and entry rules are identical; both labels must be resolved from the common path.

## Required synthetic design

1. Generate minute-level paths for a fixed synthetic 20-symbol universe over a frozen chronological span. Add shared market, symbol, timeframe, oscillator, and event-level dependence; aggregate the same underlying minute path to the five native timeframes. Register the exact dependence parameters before the full run.
2. Generate signal issuance causally by running the frozen divergence detector on the synthetic OHLC paths; do not substitute an abstract hot/cold issue process for the production detector. RSI/MACD and timeframes may issue overlapping events at a common timestamp. Count unique forecast IDs separately for each oscillator×timeframe×horizon claim. Coverage is that claim's issued IDs divided by all scheduled frozen-universe symbol×completed-native-bar opportunities for its fold, including no-signal, macro-unavailable, and feed-failure opportunities. Store the pre-outcome issued-ID ledger; a planted dropped-ID negative control must lower coverage without shrinking the denominator. Keep every emitted, adjudicable overlapping signal in primary M2; report unresolved paths separately, and mark a claim INCONCLUSIVE when path resolution is below 95%. Report `wins/resolved` as resolved-issued M2 and `wins/emitted` with every unresolved issue treated as failure as a separate sensitivity. The outcome-blind non-overlap sample is used only for N≥200 and symbol-diversity eligibility; it never replaces either M2 denominator.
3. At every emitted anchor, resolve candidate and both baselines with the frozen execution, fees, stop-first path convention, and claim-specific geometry. Candidate and baselines share the anchor and the same underlying minute path; baselines use their preregistered sides and common next-open entry. Compute 10-bar and 20-bar outcomes from their common path; do not independently draw horizon labels. For fixed geometry, a target reached within 10 bars must also be recorded within 20 bars, but separately selected horizon claims need not have identical geometry. Every synthetic panel must execute all five fixed folds (train/test UTC boundaries: 2022-01-01/2023-05-20/2024-01-27; 2022-09-10/2024-01-27/2024-10-05; 2023-05-20/2024-10-05/2025-06-14; 2024-01-27/2025-06-14/2026-02-21; 2024-10-05/2026-02-21/2026-10-31), 504/252-day WFA, and a 23-native-bar train purge. In each training fold, evaluate all 48 cells and apply the frozen preregistration exactly: M2≥80%, ≥30 resolved signals, positive base-cost net expectancy, ≥95% path resolution, plateau neighbor pass fraction≥80% and selected-vs-neighbor median difference≤10 percentage points, then tie-break by higher M2, higher expectancy, and the registered lexicographic order. No qualifying cell means no selected policy for that fold. Freeze the selected parameters in test; censor any event whose full activation/outcome interval crosses the exclusive fold end. Aggregate each claim's issued OOS ledger across the five folds without pooling claims; test-path mutation must not change train selection.
4. For claim `c`, define the null as `H0,c: Δ[c,momentum]≤0 OR Δ[c,SHA]≤0`; promotion requires both paired effects to be positive, so a claim is non-null only when both are positive. The global paired-only null is that all 20 claims are null; the complete equality special case has all 40 contrasts exactly zero. Calibrate false claim-level superiority without the M2≥80 gate under that global null and preregistered partial-null profiles. Keep candidate and both baseline outcomes on the same simulated paths. Verify the fixed SHA-hash direction is balanced across synthetic issued anchors and independent of future path innovations; do not redraw it per panel. Report full-rule false promotions as diagnostics, not as a separate acceptance gate. For each panel, the set of full-rule false-promoted claims must be a subset of paired-only superiority claims intersected with that panel's registered true-null claim set, using the identical claim family, eligibility mask, OOS selection, block scales, and lower bounds; assert this exact relation. A complete equality null with candidate and SHA-fair both at M2≥80 is structurally inactive under SHA's mirrored same-entry long/short barriers with target-R≥1: for each path, at most one side reaches target before the opposite stop, so the fair side's expected target-first rate is ≤50%. Do not condition on high realized M2 or inject labels to bypass this bound. Strong error control is claimed only over the exact registered partial-null layouts tested; every registered profile must retain its true-null claims in the 40-contrast max-T family. No partial-null layout may be selected after results.
5. Test missing-path rates at 0%, 1% MCAR, 4% MCAR, 5% boundary, 6% above-gate, and a volatility-clustered 4% case. The preregistered rule is resolved-issued primary M2 only when path resolution is at least 95%, plus all-issued unresolved-as-failure sensitivity. Any claim below 95% must be INCONCLUSIVE; no missing signal may be deleted from issue/coverage accounting.
6. Run the frozen R019 overlapping-block multiplier max-T construction across all 40 contrasts, preserving the complete contrast vector at each shared time block. Use block lengths 1, 2, 5, 10, and 20 days; a claim's paired superiority requires both baseline one-sided 95% lower bounds to exceed zero at every block length. Test the per-panel full-rule false-promotion subset invariant separately from paired-only family error; full-rule false-promotion rate is diagnostic, not an independent gate. Record simultaneous coverage, each error event, claim eligibility, and power. Use independent panel seeds and 1,999 synchronized draws per panel/scale. The Wilson 95% upper bound for paired-only familywise false-superiority rate over registered true-null claims must be ≤6%; ≥95% of panels must satisfy the preregistered all-claims eligibility rule where applicable. Record every seed, source/config hash, run status, and Monte Carlo error. Never treat synthetic calibration results as market strategy evidence.

## Candidate acceptance gates for the design review

- 5,000 independently seeded panels per predeclared null/partial-null dependence profile; 1,999 synchronized bootstrap draws per panel and each frozen block scale.
- For each profile, the global-null and partial-null paired-only family-wise false-superiority rates have Wilson 95% upper bounds ≤6%, calculated over the true-null claims; partial alternatives must not remove null claims from the max-T family. Full-rule false-promotion rates are reported separately and must satisfy the per-panel subset assertion, but are not an independent acceptance gate. Simultaneous one-sided lower-bound family coverage has a 95% Wilson lower bound ≥94%. A complete equality-null full-rule rate is reported as STRUCTURALLY INACTIVE, not counted as a pass. With no missing paths, ≥95% of panels must have all 20 claims eligible; under 1% and 4% missingness the same eligibility gate applies. The exact 5% boundary reports eligibility without presuming a pass; 6% missingness must leave affected claims INCONCLUSIVE. At 5,000 panels the Wilson 95% half-width for any error rate with observed rate ≤6% must be ≤0.7 percentage points. No numerical/runtime errors are permitted.
- Simultaneous interval coverage, Monte Carlo error, replay determinism, missingness boundaries, all-issued/resolved-issued denominators, and non-overlap eligibility pass explicit tests; no numerical/runtime errors.
- The 4% missingness profiles must never promote a claim whose realized path resolution is below 95%; the 6% stress profile must be blocked as INCONCLUSIVE. The exact 5% profile is a boundary diagnostic and reports its eligible fraction, not an assumed pass rate. Missing, no-signal, and macro-unavailable scheduled opportunities remain in issue-coverage accounting.
- An independent red-team review approves the generator and its test coverage before any full simulation. Failure means redesign the method and preregister a new version; it does not authorize market-label access.
- Benchmark a synthetic-only end-to-end pilot before the full campaign. The pilot must run the same WFA, selection, purge, path resolution, inference, and output path as the final panels, and report wall time, peak memory, deterministic replay, and per-stage costs. The 5,000-panel/1,999-draw gates are not reduced to fit runtime; if infeasible, optimize the implementation or preregister a scientifically justified alternative before running it.

## Unresolved design decisions before preregistration is locked

- The complete 30-second latent return law and fixed parameters for A/B/C profiles, OHLC construction, and causal injection mechanism for the active partial-null layout.
- Whether the resulting real detector issues enough events for per-claim coverage/N eligibility; this must be measured outcome-blind in the synthetic pilot and cannot be repaired by deleting no-signal opportunities.
- The joint dependence parameters and complete-null/partial-null path laws, including a perfectly duplicated-claim negative control.
- Whether the planned panel count and bootstrap count are computationally feasible at the stated dimensions; this requires the end-to-end benchmark above and cannot be inferred from R019's narrower vectorized kernel.
- How common path segments are represented efficiently while retaining the dependence between oscillator streams, nested timeframes, and overlapping 10/20-bar horizons.

## Red-team interpretation of the null and interval gates

The paired-only family error is the primary size test: a claim is null when at least one of its two candidate-minus-baseline effects is nonpositive, and false claim superiority means both lower bounds clear zero at every block scale. The global null has all 20 claims null; partial-null profiles may have positive contrasts but must retain every null claim in the 40-contrast max-T family. Under partial-null profiles, a full-rule false promotion is a promoted true-null claim; its set must be a subset of paired-only superiority claims intersected with the registered true-null set, using identical panel-level eligibility, claim selection, contrasts, scales, and bounds. A complete equality null in which candidate and SHA-fair have expected M2≥80% is not a valid synthetic stress case: under the baseline's mirrored same-entry long/short barriers with target-R≥1, at most one side can hit target before the other side's stop, so a fair path-independent SHA side has expected M2≤50%. Do not condition on unusually high realized SHA M2 or inject labels to bypass this bound. Strong error control under partial alternatives is claimed only for the exact registered profiles tested.

SHA direction uses the preregistered deterministic key and issued forecast IDs; do not redraw it by panel. Test the hash-side balance and independence from future innovations on the exact synthetic issuance ledger. For each profile/scale, simultaneous one-sided lower-bound coverage is the event that all 40 registered true contrasts are covered in the correct direction; report its Wilson interval, with a 95% Wilson lower bound of at least 94%. At 5,000 panels, report Wilson 95% Monte Carlo half-width (≤0.7 percentage points when the observed error rate is ≤6%). The full family decision is the registered intersection over block lengths 1, 2, 5, 10, and 20 days; do not select a scale after results.

Until the complete joint path/issuance law, partial-null mechanism, and same-pipeline benchmark are fixed and independently approved, do not run a full calibration, divergence market backtest, or signal promotion.

## Independent DGP proposal review — 2026-11-19

The independent researcher returned an implementation-level proposal without reading market labels or changing code. Its concrete values are **synthetic stress assumptions, not empirical estimates**:

- 20 correlated symbols at 30-second resolution, using common-market, symbol-specific, common-volatility, and symbol-specific-volatility AR(1) factors. Return innovations combine factor variance weights 0.35/0.25/0.40 with variance-normalized Student-t(5) idiosyncratic noise. Baseline volatility is 0.05% per 30 seconds and log-volatility is stochastic.
- Dependence profiles A/B/C use `(phi_market, phi_symbol, phi_vol)=(0,0,0)`, `(0.50,0.25,0.80)`, and `(0.70,0.35,0.95)`. OHLC is formed from six conditioned 5-second bridge increments, then aggregated to 1-minute and native bars. Synthetic starts, volume, factor weights, volatility, bridge construction, and all AR values are assumptions selected for simulation, not estimated from a registered market sample.
- Candidate drift is injected after issued signals for up to 20 native bars, scaled by volatility and split over the active interval, summed across overlapping signals, then capped to ±0.25 volatility units. `theta=0.25` and the cap are also arbitrary. This creates stochastic alternatives but does **not** prove which of the 20 claims are truly null; effects leak across oscillators and nested timeframes through shared paths.
- The proposal estimates about 103.6 million 30-second bars per panel, or 518 billion bar updates for 5,000 panels, before WFA/48-cell selection and outcome resolution. A rough unbenchmarked throughput range alone spans about 14 hours to 6 days for path generation; the max-T draw workload adds substantial processing. This is arithmetic, not a runtime measurement.

**Red-team decision: do not adopt these assumptions as the locked DGP and do not launch the full simulation.** The proposal itself says no market outcome was examined and no code was run. Three items still fail the preregistration gate: (1) the synthetic symbol identities/order/hash are not specified (the source market-data audit's 86-symbol archive is not itself a 20-symbol synthetic cohort); (2) no mathematical construction establishes the true-null claim set for global/partial-null profiles, especially with candidate stop-entry versus baseline next-open entries; and (3) no same-pipeline pilot provides measured runtime or memory. The suggested signal-conditioned drift is a power/overlap stress mechanism only; it cannot support a claim of strong partial-null FWER when true-null membership is unknown. Do not infer an edge, an 80% target-first rate, or a pass from this proposal.

The next valid step is a preregistered theoretical construction for testable null/alternative masks, then an outcome-blind implementation pilot on a small fixed synthetic fixture that exercises the exact detector, WFA, selection, and inference path. A fixture may verify causality and implementation invariants; it may not be presented as calibration or strategy evidence. If a valid mixed-null law cannot be constructed for the real detector, report partial-null strong control as unavailable and decide whether global-null-only calibration is methodologically sufficient **before** running a campaign.

### Follow-up red-team finding — existing calibration code

The follow-up implementation audit distinguishes existing machinery from the missing R020 evidence:

- R018 (`scripts/research/crypto_r018_paired_baseline_calibration.py`) generates abstract paired labels with assigned equal-rate nulls and predefined alternatives. R019 (`scripts/research/crypto_r019_block_multiplier_calibration.py`) calibrates block-multiplier inference on those contrasts. R019-P (`scripts/research/crypto_r019_power_missingness.py`) assigns partial-null labels and evaluates those abstract panels.
- These generators do not derive signals from the frozen divergence detector on one shared multi-symbol OHLC path, and do not model the candidate stop-entry versus baseline next-open execution plus overlapping 10/20-bar outcomes. Their prior rates therefore cannot establish R020's claim truth, size, or power.
- Red-team conclusion is **not** that a valid mixed-null generator is mathematically impossible. It is that no implementation-grounded construction or proof currently establishes the requested signs under the frozen end-to-end pipeline. A proposed profile without that proof is an unverified stress case, not a true-null calibration.
- The narrowest defensible future claim is limited to named synthetic path distributions for which each tested claim's null status is independently established under the full frozen detector/WFA/execution process. Such results cannot be generalized to the full composite global claim null, arbitrary partial-null configurations, or other path distributions. If independent null certification is not feasible, partial-null strong control must be reported as unavailable; global-null-only calibration is a separate methodological decision and must be preregistered before outcomes are generated.

No market outcomes were opened, no code was executed, and no R020 experiment was run in this review.
