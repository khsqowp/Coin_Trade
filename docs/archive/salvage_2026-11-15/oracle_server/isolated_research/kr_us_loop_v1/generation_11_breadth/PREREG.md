# Generation 11 breadth preregistration — sector participation gate for SPY

Status: frozen candidate proposal; performance data must not be read or scored before independent reviewer GO.

## Hypothesis and prior overlap

Hypothesis: a broad advance across legacy US sectors may distinguish durable broad-market risk-on states from narrow participation. Use the fraction of nine fixed SPDR sector ETFs trading above their own 200-session simple moving average (SMA200) as an equal-count market-internals signal; hold SPY only when at least a strict majority of the nine sectors are above their own trend. This is a transfer hypothesis, not a claim that sector breadth predicts returns.

This candidate is explicitly related to prior breadth/timing work, not an independent strategy family. Generation 02 already tested `BREADTH_60_40_BASKET`: monthly PIT-stock breadth over each stock's SMA200 with 60/40 hysteresis, then an equal-weight stock basket or cash. US v2 X2 used SPY relative to SMA200 as a cash gate; US v2 X9 used individual-stock SMA50/SMA200 conditions; Gen01 also included a broad-market 200SMA regime switch. The current rule differs in its fixed nine-sector ETF breadth input, daily close decision, non-hysteretic majority threshold, and SPY/cash exposure output. These differences do not erase family-wise contamination. The current SPY history and fixed ETF histories have already been examined in prior generations; any eventual result is exploratory and not untouched OOS.

## Frozen rule

1. **Universe and inputs:** exactly XLB, XLE, XLF, XLI, XLK, XLP, XLU, XLV, and XLY for breadth; SPY for the traded portfolio and benchmark. Read only `../data_mirror/us_daily/{ticker}.parquet`. Do not query remote/server data or alter the snapshot. Sector ETF histories are the observed legacy funds, not a point-in-time constituent universe and not proof of sector-index continuity.
2. **Breadth calculation:** at each observed session close `t`, for each of the nine ETFs compute `SMA200_i[t]` as the arithmetic mean of its 200 adjusted closes ending at and including `t`. A sector is above trend iff `AdjClose_i[t] > SMA200_i[t]`; equality is not above. Breadth is the integer count of above-trend sectors. All nine sector closes must be finite and positive and all nine SMAs must be available; otherwise the attempt fails, without dropping an ETF or reducing the denominator.
3. **Exposure rule:** after close `t`, target exposure is 100% SPY if breadth is at least 5 of 9; otherwise target is 0% cash. No hysteresis, persistence, alternative threshold, weighting, leverage, shorting, stop, or intra-day decision is allowed. The SMA window includes the decision close, and the completed close signal first takes effect at the next observed open `t+1`.
4. **Price and accounting:** derive adjusted open per row as `raw_open * adjclose / raw_close`; adjusted close is the supplied `adjclose`. Daily portfolio return is adjusted-open-to-adjusted-open while exposure is held. Apply 10 bp multiplicative cost per unit of absolute exposure change at each open (100% entry/exit = 10 bp; unchanged exposure = zero). Charge a final 10 bp liquidation cost at the last observed open if invested. Cash return is zero. No taxes, interest, market impact or borrow.
5. **Benchmark:** SPY buy-and-hold on exactly the same open-to-open interval dates, with the same initial 10 bp entry and terminal 10 bp liquidation charges. Do not reset wealth at descriptive-window boundaries. The primary comparisons are net CAGR and annualized daily Sharpe; report MDD, cumulative return, exposure and switches as context.
6. **Observation windows:** full available common history after the first valid SMA200 signal; fixed descriptive partitions 2001-01-01 through 2016-12-31 and 2017-01-01 through the final complete observed interval. These are previously inspected dates, not untouched OOS. Do not create a training/tuning partition from them.
7. **Decision:** one candidate, one run, no parameter variants. Reject this fixed rule for the stated search objective if net CAGR and net Sharpe do not both exceed matched SPY buy-and-hold in every reported window. If descriptive comparisons pass, retain only as a lead pending licensed corporate-action/sector data, a genuinely prospective holdout, and full cumulative-trial/multiplicity validation. Either outcome is only about this fixed rule and snapshot; it cannot establish whether other profitable systematic strategies exist.

## Data integrity and limitations

Before reporting metrics, require all ten parquet files to exist, contain the required `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `adjclose` columns, have unique strictly ascending and exactly identical date indexes, finite positive prices, and valid raw OHLC bounds. Fail closed; no forward-filling, repair, row dropping, or silently changing the breadth denominator. Require at least 200 full common observations plus at least one subsequent open-to-open interval. The Yahoo-derived local feed, retrospective adjusted prices, ETF corporate-action history, true auction fills, and authoritative exchange calendar are not independently verified. The prior generations have already examined these histories, so there is no untouched historical test period available in this snapshot.

No DSR/PSR/BH or other multiplicity-adjusted significance assertion is planned for this exploratory candidate. The exact global historical trial count is unresolved, and this candidate is dependent on already explored breadth/trend-gate families. A run can reject only this fixed rule; a descriptive pass is not a validated edge.
