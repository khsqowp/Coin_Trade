# Generation 11 cross-asset preregistration — one-month sector-ETF reversal

Registered: 2026-11-06 (Asia/Seoul). This is one exploratory US multi-ETF strategy test, not a confirmatory or investable result. It does not imply that failure of this candidate means no profitable automated strategy exists.

## Candidate and rationale

Test one long-only, cross-sectional short-term reversal rule on the nine legacy SPDR US sector ETFs: XLB, XLE, XLF, XLI, XLK, XLP, XLU, XLV, XLY. At each completed calendar month-end, rank ETFs by the return in the immediately preceding completed calendar month; hold the three lowest-return sectors equally weighted for the next calendar month. This is a simple sector-ETF adaptation of the documented one-month return-reversal research (Jegadeesh, 1990, DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.1990.tb05110.x; primary-paper landing page: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1990.tb05110.x). That paper studies securities, not these ETFs. The aggregate-sector adaptation is an unverified hypothesis, not a claim that the published stock-level effect transfers.

## Prior-work overlap / novelty boundary

Existing US v1/v2 work already examined sector momentum/rotation, 200-session trend timing, volatility targeting, and SPY/QQQ/sector ETF behavior; the exact previous rules include cross-sectional momentum ranks and sector rotation. Generations 01–10 also include a weekly bottom-50 five-session stock reversal (US v2 X6), and other reversal/trend candidates. No earlier preregistration or result located in this bounded audit specifies the exact rule below: rank the nine sector ETFs by the immediately preceding calendar month's total return and long the bottom three for the following month. The new test is therefore a distinct universe/horizon implementation within the already-explored short-term reversal family, not an independent alpha family and not a clean replication. Any result will be treated as exploratory and correlated with prior reversal/sector research.

## Frozen universe, signal, execution, and costs

1. **Universe:** exactly the nine symbols listed above; no SPY, QQQ, XLC, XLRE, later-added sector, single-stock constituent, changing universe, sector relabeling, short position, leverage, or cash substitute. The local mirror lacks XLC and XLRE. No survivorship-free sector classification claim is made; SPDR sector definitions and fund histories are used as the fixed observed ETF universe.
2. **Inputs:** read only the local `data_mirror/us_daily/{ticker}.parquet` snapshot. Expected columns are `open`, `high`, `low`, `close`, `adjclose`, `volume`, indexed by date. Do not query Oracle or any live system. All nine sector indexes must be unique, ascending, and exactly equal to one another. Any missing file, non-finite/non-positive required OHLC/adjusted-close value, invalid OHLC relationship, duplicate/nonascending date, or index mismatch fails the full attempt; do not drop or repair rows. SPY must have valid bars on the exact sector dates for benchmark comparison.
3. **Adjustment:** adjusted close is the source's `adjclose`. For execution PnL, adjusted open is `open * adjclose / close`, consistent with the prior project convention. Raw OHLC validity is checked; no intraday fill is inferred. Vendor adjustment provenance, restatements, distributions, and actual execution prices are not independently verified.
4. **Signal:** for each completed calendar month m, let `R[s,m] = AdjClose[s,last_session(m)] / AdjClose[s,last_session(m-1)] - 1`; thus the just-ended month m is the most recent completed return available at the month-end decision. Rank the nine finite returns ascending; ticker symbol ascending is the exact deterministic tie-break. Select exactly the three lowest-return ETFs, equally weighted 1/3 each. No threshold, eligibility exception, volatility adjustment, absolute-return filter, or alternate number of holdings. Skip the first sector-observation month (December 1998) because the file begins partway through it; skip the final observed calendar month (November 2026) because it is incomplete. For the first eligible decision, the return month must be fully represented in the file.
5. **Decision/fill:** calculate the selection only after the last observed close of month m. Enter or rebalance at the next observed session's adjusted open, which begins exposure for month m+1. Hold those target weights until the open following the next completed month-end decision. There is one scheduled rebalance per completed month; no intramonth signal, stop, discretionary adjustment, or overlapping book. Do not initiate a target for a holding month that is the final incomplete calendar month in the file; record that planned target as terminal-censored, keep the prior holdings through the last observed open, and liquidate them there. This avoids presenting an incomplete holding month as a normal monthly observation.
6. **Daily portfolio accounting:** between adjacent observed opens, earn each held ETF's adjusted-open-to-adjusted-open simple return using weights established at the earlier open. Let weights drift with relative asset returns between monthly trades. At each rebalance open, target the three new sectors at 1/3 each, with zero cash; compute turnover as the sum of absolute changes in risky-asset weights after drift. Apply 10 bp cost per unit of traded notional on both buys and sells, equivalently `cost = 0.001 * sum(abs(new_risky_weights - drifted_risky_weights))`, multiplicatively to wealth at that open. No cash interest, market impact model, tax, leverage, or shorting. Liquidate at the final observed open with the same 10 bp/sell-notional charge. A runner must make these timing and drift rules explicit and unit-tested before the market performance run.
7. **Benchmark:** SPY buy-and-hold on precisely the sector common dates and adjusted-open series, entering at the first available open and liquidating at the last available open, with 10 bp entry and 10 bp exit costs. Use the same dates and a continuous wealth path; do not restart portfolio state or manufacture trades at descriptive-window boundaries. Primary comparison is net CAGR and annualized daily Sharpe against SPY. Report maximum drawdown, exposure, turnover, number of monthly decisions and holdings as context.
8. **Descriptive windows:** full common sample; 2001-01-01 through 2016-12-31; 2017-01-01 through 2026-11-02. Each is a slice of the continuously simulated portfolios, and not untouched OOS. Existing project history has already inspected this market snapshot. No p-value, DSR/PSR, multiple-testing significance, or deployment claim will be made because the complete historical test count, PIT fund-selection process, corporate actions, and pristine holdout are unresolved.
9. **Single-candidate budget / decision:** one candidate, one run, no parameter variants or post-result retuning. If it fails to beat SPY on both net CAGR and net Sharpe in every fixed displayed window, reject this fixed rule for the stated objective. If it beats those descriptive metrics, retain it only as a lead for independent validated-data and prospective holdout testing. Either outcome says only whether this frozen candidate merits further work; it does not establish or refute existence of other profitable strategies.

## Data facts recorded before performance evaluation

The local files exist for all nine sector ETFs and SPY. Each sector ETF file contains 7,008 rows from 1998-12-22 through 2026-11-02; SPY contains 8,498 rows from 1993-01-29 through 2026-11-02. Each sector file was observed to have the same row count, index start/end, unique ascending dates, and expected OHLCV/adjusted-close columns; full bar integrity is a fail-closed runner gate. The source mirror is a non-atomic Yahoo-derived snapshot, not an authoritative total-return/point-in-time database. These facts establish file availability only, not strategy performance or valid exchange-calendar completeness.
