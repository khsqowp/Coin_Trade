### Gen07 input-coverage audit — US overnight/daytime candidate

The local PIT component snapshot has 2,720 date rows from 1996-01-02 through 2026-08-18. A read-only audit matched each month-end member list to local parquet bars and counted a name as usable only if it had at least 15 same-month positive, non-null open/close observations. Under that explicit screen, monthly usable-member coverage has median 65.8% and range 37.0%–99.0% over 368 months; only 81 months meet 85% coverage, beginning 2019-12 and ending 2026-08.

A second pre-run feature-availability pass applied the paper's monthly signal structure to available `open`, `close`, and `adjclose` fields: at least 15 valid decomposition observations in month `t`, plus at least 10 computable monthly `NR` values in `t-12..t-1` and a positive prior mean. It found 79 of 368 months meeting the same 85% PIT-member coverage gate, from 2020-02 through 2026-08; median coverage was 65.8%, range 36.1%–98.4%. This was a feature-availability diagnostic only. The corrected Gen07 rerun later counted the entire active PIT membership, excluded the trailing partial month, and found 83 of 406 completed decisions meeting the gate (Dec 2019–Oct 2026; median coverage 63.9%). Price adjustment semantics, corporate actions, exact historical security identity, and tradable delisting proceeds remain unvalidated; see `generation_07/RESULTS.md` for the exploratory return test.

The candidate therefore cannot currently support a long historical confirmation: the signal-complete subset passing the 85% screen spans only about 6.5 years, all of which has already been repeatedly inspected in this research effort. A future-only holdout is not present in the snapshot. Do not mask this by shrinking the universe, silently removing unavailable names, or interpreting this exploratory interval as untouched OOS. The audit does not test returns and does not reject the economic hypothesis.

The candidate's literature basis is Akbas, Boehmer, Jiang, and Koch, *Overnight returns, daytime reversals, and future stock returns*, Journal of Financial Economics 145(3), 2022. Their paper defines the abnormal frequency of positive overnight returns followed by negative daytime returns, reports a positive cross-sectional association with next-month returns in its CRSP sample, and reports that the predictive return is concentrated in the long leg. The paper also finds no comparable relation for the opposite reversal sequence. These are published-sample results, not evidence of profitability in this repository's data or after our costs. [Author working-paper PDF](https://assets.super.so/e46b77e7-ee08-445e-b43f-4ffd88ae0a0e/files/1cbfd6c5-9341-4266-95f9-56b95d71e905.pdf) · [Journal article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2021.09.019).

The independent Gen07 reviewers also found that daily advance–decline breadth thrust, CHOP-conditioned switching, new-high/new-low breadth, year-end loser reversal, and overnight-return ranking are technically definable but adjacent to already explored breadth, momentum, reversal, or calendar families. They remain untested leads. Earnings-announcement, PEAD, and analyst-revision strategies remain infeasible without point-in-time event/consensus timestamps. No performance runs were executed by the Gen07 reviewers.

### 2026-11-06 — KR price-limit event lead: paper effect is not daily-bar executable

A primary KCI article provides a market-specific mechanism not represented by the generic US trend rules: on the KRX, a stock closing exactly at its upper or lower daily price limit may have delayed price discovery, and the paper studies close-to-next-open continuation. Its 2007–2009 KOSPI sample reports positive statistically significant market-adjusted returns only for price-limit-hit events and stronger risk-adjusted results for upper-limit than lower-limit events. The study excludes new listings, delistings, and managed stocks, uses a period before Korea's 2015 expansion from ±15% to ±30%, and does not establish present-day tradable performance. [Choi & Han (2010), KCI article and PDF](https://journal.kci.go.kr/kiri/archive/articleView?artiId=ART001496198).

This is **not yet an executable daily-OHLCV strategy**. The paper's measured payoff interval begins at the limit close and ends at the next open. A strategy that observes the close at the limit cannot then retroactively buy at that close; a preplaced order's fill and queue priority are not inferable from daily OHLCV. ### 2026-11-07 — nominal share-price cross-section: additional low-priority hypothesis

A separate primary-literature screen identified nominal share price as a cross-sectional characteristic not specified as a candidate in the bounded ledger.
